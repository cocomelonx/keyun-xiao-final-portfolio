About the project

01 / 09

A coffee bar that serves its menu the way a quartet plays — one body of work, released in four movements.

Monolith is a specialty shop and roastery on East Valley Boulevard in Alhambra, pouring drinks with names like Five Moons and Tiger in Tweed in a room where boulders hang over an ash counter. The identity takes the name literally and then softens it: the mark is a figure leaning into a stone, the same weight as the rocks in the room. Every surface — cup, poster, feed — carries that one silhouette.

The system is bilingual by default. English carries the drink, Chinese carries the chapter, and neither is a translation of the other.

The menu turns with the year — 序 still, 雨讯 monsoon, 日曜 solstice, 霜降 frost.

印

The mark

02 / 09

A figure and a boulder in the same silhouette: a round head set off the shoulder, a back that falls away in one long curve, legs cut from the mass as negative space. Drawn in a single weight with no outline, so it holds at a 40px avatar and at the scale of a shopfront. The head reads as a stone from across the street and as a face once you are at the counter.

Stacked for the cup, where the wordmark has to survive a sleeve and a lid.

色

Palette

03 / 09

Basalt and chalk do the structural work. The warm half of the range is pulled off the counter — crema, clay, the oat-milk haze in a cup held to the window — and only ever appears as a gradient field, never as a flat fill.

字

Typography

04 / 09
32° M outer stroke 30° N diagonal 1 : 1 O is a true circle 3 heights, centred on one axis

The wordmark is drawn rather than set. Its obliques hold to roughly 32° on the M and 30° on the N, the O is a true circle, and the letters are centred on a common axis rather than seated on a baseline — M, N, I and H sit short against the full-height L and T, with the O overshooting both. The angles above are measured off the artwork, not assigned to it. Menu and campaign copy are set in a plain grotesque so the drawn letterforms stay the only voice.
